Genocide in Palestine: the UN finding for Gaza – and the West Bank

No longer an open “question” but a finding: in September 2025 the UN Commission of Inquiry concluded that Israeli authorities and security forces have committed and are continuing to commit genocide in the Gaza Strip – a court judgment is still pending. What does the Genocide Convention require – and what has the United Nations Commission of Inquiry found, act by act? With the paragraph references in the report, the opposing positions and the status of all proceedings in November 2026.

The key points first

Four of five acts – and the intent

The finding of the UN Commission of Inquiry

The United Nations Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel concluded in September 2025 that Israeli authorities and security forces in the Gaza Strip have committed and are continuing to commit genocide. It established four of the five acts of the Genocide Convention – killing, serious harm, destructive conditions of life, prevention of births – and the intent to destroy, in whole or in part, the Palestinians in Gaza. This intent, it said, was “the only reasonable inference” from the totality of the evidence.

The Commission presented the result not only in a detailed conference room paper (A/HRC/60/CRP.3, 16 September 2025), but also in its regular report to the UN General Assembly (A/80/337, 14 August 2025, paras. 66–70).

What this is – and what it is not
  • 1Not a court judgment. The Commission applies the standard of “reasonable grounds”. Only the International Court of Justice can deliver a judgment – in the case South Africa v. Israel, after 2029 at the earliest.
  • 2Israel rejects the finding and called the report “distorted and false”.
  • 3The fifth act – the forcible transfer of children – was not established by the Commission.

Not only Gaza

And the West Bank? One people, two arenas

The UN Commission's genocide finding concerns the Gaza Strip. But the same Commission, the UN Special Rapporteur and Israeli human rights organisations see the same direction in the West Bank – and, for prisoners in Israeli custody, conditions confirmed by Israel's own courts and authorities.

Gaza – genocide found

UN Commission of Inquiry (September 2025, confirmed October 2026): genocide, four of five prohibited acts, intent as “the only reasonable inference”; B'Tselem, Amnesty and the genocide scholars' association IAGS reach the same conclusion. → Finding above · → Gaza

West Bank – crimes against humanity

On 26 October 2026 the UN Commission for the first time found crimes against humanity there: extermination, torture, rape and persecution, as a “widespread and systematic attack” by the army and settlers. 254 people killed in airstrikes since October 2023 – compared with 6 in the 15 years before. Chair Muralidhar: part of a “broader strategy to exterminate and displace Palestinians” (Al Jazeera, via the press; report A/81/430). There is no genocide finding for the West Bank so far. → West Bank

In Israeli custody

At least 98 deaths in custody since October 2023 (PHRI); autopsies show violence and neglect, a 17-year-old starved. Israel's Supreme Court found on 7 September 2025 that the state does not feed the prisoners adequately; the Prison Service itself reported 1,704 active scabies cases. → Detention (in German)

Who applies the genocide framework to all of Palestine
  • 1UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese (“Genocide as colonial erasure”, A/79/384, October 2024): the violence against Palestinians as a group warrants applying the Genocide Convention across the entire occupied territory (A/79/384).
  • 2B'Tselem warned in July 2025 (“Our Genocide”) that Israel is reproducing Gaza's patterns in the West Bank on a smaller scale; in September 2026 it described an “elimination project” there (Al Jazeera, via the press).
  • 3Assessment: The Convention protects a group “in whole or in part” – Palestinians are one group wherever they live. Whether the acts in the West Bank are part of the same genocide has not yet been determined by any body; the question is open and is being asked.
No judgment does not mean: no genocide

Why the absence of an ICJ judgment does not refute the findings

Key statement

A UN Commission of Inquiry, the UN Special Rapporteur, a UN committee, the major human rights organisations – two of them Israeli – and the professional association of genocide scholars have, after assessing evidence, reached the same conclusion: a genocide is being committed in Gaza. That the International Court of Justice has not yet ruled is due to the length of such proceedings – not to the evidence. Anyone who wants to dispute the findings must refute the evidence; pointing to a pending judgment is not enough.

There is no resolution of the UN General Assembly, the Security Council or the Human Rights Council with a finding of genocide.

How the bodies proceeded

They examined each act of the Genocide Convention (Art. II a–e) individually against cases, figures and witness testimony. For the decisive intent to destroy, the UN Commission (CRP.3, para. 254), Albanese (A/HRC/55/73, para. 57) and Amnesty expressly applied the ICJ’s own standard: the intent must be the only reasonable inference from the pattern of conduct. The basis is statements by Israeli officials and the pattern of actions – the same kind of evidence that a court also examines.

Why there is no judgment yet
  • 1Bosnia v. Serbia: application 1993, judgment 2007 – almost 14 years.
  • 2Croatia v. Serbia: 1999 to 2015 – 15.5 years.
  • 3The Gambia v. Myanmar: application 2019; judgment on 19 November 2026 – after 7 years: Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya (ICJ press release).
  • 4South Africa v. Israel: application at the end of 2023; Israel’s final pleading is not due until 22 May 2029. A judgment is therefore hardly to be expected before 2030 (own estimate based on the comparable cases).

No genocide case before the ICJ has so far taken less than seven years (case 91, 118, 178, 192).

What the ICJ ordered in 2024 – precisely

In three orders (26 January, 28 March, 24 May 2024) the Court found “a real and imminent risk that irreparable prejudice” would be caused to the right of the Palestinians in Gaza to be protected from genocide, and obliged Israel to take protective measures. For the facts it relied on UN sources, including a statement by 37 UN Special Rapporteurs (paras. 46–53). Former President Joan Donoghue made clear that whether Israel has violated the Convention will be decided “at a later stage of the proceedings” (translated from German). This means: the question is open – it has been decided neither against nor in favour of Israel. The widespread claim that the ICJ declared a genocide “plausible” is inaccurate: what it called plausible was the right to protection.

The obligation applies already now

The Genocide Convention requires not only punishment but prevention. The ICJ ruled in 2007 that this obligation of every State arises at the instant the State learns of the existence of a “serious risk that genocide will be committed” – not only with a judgment. It would, the Court said, be absurd if the obligation arose only once the genocide itself had begun (Bosnia judgment, para. 431). Since the order of 26 January 2024, no State can claim ignorance any longer (UN Commission, CRP.3 para. 249).

From historyIt was the same in Rwanda and Bosnia

18 Dec 1992

Bosnia: The UN General Assembly calls “ethnic cleansing” a form of genocide (Res. 47/121) – more than 14 years before the ICJ judgment.

28 Jun 1994

Rwanda: After an eleven-day mission, UN Special Rapporteur René Degni-Ségui finds that “the term ‘genocide’ should henceforth be used as regards the Tutsi” (E/CN.4/1995/7, para. 48). In October and December 1994 a UN Commission of Experts confirms the finding.

2 Sep 1998

First judgment by an international court for genocide (Rwanda Tribunal, Akayesu) – more than four years after the finding by the UN Special Rapporteur. To this day there is no ICJ judgment on Rwanda, because no State brought a case; yet nobody disputes the genocide there.

26 Feb 2007

ICJ on Srebrenica: The Court confirms the genocide and relies expressly on UN reports; it attributes “considerable authority” (translated from German) to the UN report on the fall of Srebrenica because of the care taken, its comprehensive sources and its independence (para. 230).

The lesson: investigative bodies regularly name a genocide years before a court does – and the courts later rely on precisely these reports. The pending judgment in the Gaza case is the normal course of events, not a sign of weak evidence.

What remains open – stated fairly
  • 1A court can decide differently: in Croatia v. Serbia (2015) the ICJ established killings but considered expulsion rather than destruction to be the aim. Its standard of proof (“fully conclusive” evidence, translated from German; Bosnia judgment para. 209) is stricter than that of the investigative bodies.
  • 2Israel calls the new report of the UN Commission a “misinformation campaign”, the USA rejects “all its baseless findings” (US Mission to the UN, 26 October 2026; Israel 27 October 2026). Germany points out that the question will be settled before the ICJ. The other side argues that the aim of the war is Hamas, not the population.
  • 3The bodies respond with the ICJ standard itself: an alternative explanation excludes intent only if it is supported by evidence – as Germany and five other States themselves argued in The Gambia v. Myanmar. The UN Commission states that it is ready to hand over its evidence to any court.
The standard

What the Genocide Convention requires

Article II

Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide of 9 December 1948; Germany has been a State party since 1954.

The wording

“In the present Convention, genocide means any of the following acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such: (a) Killing members of the group; (b) Causing serious bodily or mental harm to members of the group; (c) Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part; (d) Imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group; (e) Forcibly transferring children of the group to another group.”

Official English text of the Convention (the German original cites the official German translation, BGBl. 1954 II p. 730)
  • 1One act is enough – but always together with the specific intent to destroy (“dolus specialis”). It is the core of every dispute.
  • 2“In whole or in part”: the whole people need not be targeted. The Commission examined the Palestinians in the Gaza Strip as part of the protected group.
  • 3Three of the five acts do not require killing – genocide can also be committed through starvation, destruction of the means of life or prevention of births.
Act by act

What the Commission found

References: A/HRC/60/CRP.3 (“Legal analysis of the conduct of Israel in Gaza pursuant to the Genocide Convention”, 16 September 2025, 72 pages), paragraph numbers checked against the original. Quotations in English are given verbatim.

a) Killing members of the groupestablished · paras. 45–50

The Commission finds that the Israeli security forces knew that their operations would cause the death of Palestinians, and that the authorities did not change the means and methods of warfare despite the known high casualty figures. Its conclusion (para. 48): the Israeli authorities “intended to kill as many Palestinians as possible”. Para. 50: the act and the intent of killing “are established”.

Further evidence: the number of dead and its sources on Gaza: the number of dead; target selection using software (“Lavender”) on Warfare. According to an analysis of a database of Israeli military intelligence by +972 Magazine, Local Call and The Guardian (21 August 2025), by May 2025 some 8,900 fighters had been recorded as killed or probably killed – out of some 53,000 dead at the time. The Israeli army called the figures “incorrect”, without presenting figures of its own.

Opposing position: Israel states that its attacks are aimed at Hamas, which entrenches itself in residential areas; it points to warnings and evacuation orders to the civilian population.

b) Serious bodily or mental harmestablished · paras. 51–82

The Commission establishes the act (para. 82): through mutilation and amputations, repeated forced displacement, torture and sexual violence in detention and the destruction of healthcare for pregnant women and mothers (para. 79). The harm need not be permanent. According to an estimate by the World Health Organization (WHO Regional Office, 2024), there were between 3,105 and 4,050 amputations from January to May 2024 alone.

Further evidence: Childhood in Gaza · Detention and torture.

c) Destructive conditions of lifeestablished · paras. 140–147

The Commission examined five strands (para. 140): attacks on civilian objects, forced displacement, starvation as a method of warfare, the total siege (no electricity, no fuel, no food, no water, no shelter) and the blocking of humanitarian aid together with attacks on health facilities. Para. 147: the act is established.

The International Court of Justice noted in its advisory opinion of 22 October 2025 that Israel blocked all aid deliveries from 2 March to 18 May 2025, and obliged Israel to allow relief operations of the United Nations, expressly including UNRWA (A/80/502). Human Rights Watch assessed the deliberate deprivation of water as the crime of extermination and as “acts of genocide” (report of 19 December 2024).

Further evidence: famine and IPC reports on UN archive · destruction on Gaza: hunger and destruction · ministers’ statements on the siege on Politicians’ statements.

Opposing position: Israel denies a policy of starvation and justifies restrictions with the pressure to release the hostages and with theft of aid by Hamas. A study by Israeli authors calculated from truck data of the COGAT authority for January to April 2024 some 3,163 kilocalories per person per day; objection: it measures imports, not distribution and consumption.

d) Measures to prevent birthsestablished · paras. 142, 148–155

The core of this finding is the Al-Basma IVF Centre in Gaza City, the largest fertility clinic in the Gaza Strip. In December 2023 a tank shell struck the free-standing, clearly labelled building; five tanks of liquid nitrogen exploded, and some 4,000 embryos and more than 1,000 sperm and egg samples were destroyed (paras. 151–153). From the location and the pattern of damage the Commission concludes that the clinic was the main target, and assesses the attack as a measure to prevent births. In addition there are the attacks on care for pregnant women with “irreversible” consequences for reproduction (para. 142). Para. 155: the act is established.

Médecins Sans Frontières analysed the data of 201 mothers in four neonatal intensive care units (June 2025 to January 2026): more than half were malnourished during pregnancy, 90 per cent of their children were born prematurely (statement of 7 May 2026). The classification as act d) comes from the Commission, not from Médecins Sans Frontières.

Opposing position: No statement by the Israeli army on the attack on the Al-Basma Centre is available to this page.

e) Transfer of children to another groupnot established

The Commission did not establish this act; its conclusions (paras. 240 and 252) expressly name only acts a) to d).

The core of the dispute

The intent

“…genocidal intent was the only reasonable inference that could be drawn from the totality of the evidence.”

UN Commission of Inquiry, A/HRC/60/CRP.3, para. 254
Two kinds of evidence

The Commission treats statements by Israeli officials as direct evidence of genocidal intent and the pattern of conduct of the warfare as circumstantial evidence (para. 254). For the regular UN report it named six key facts, including the unprecedented number of people killed, the targeted action against children, siege and starvation, the systematic destruction of the healthcare system and the continuation of the strategy despite the warnings of the International Court of Justice (A/80/337, para. 67).

The State of Israel, it said, bears responsibility for the commission of, the failure to prevent and the failure to punish genocide (para. 255).

Incitement – the names

The Commission sees in statements by President Isaac Herzog, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and then Defence Minister Yoav Gallant “direct and public incitement to commit genocide” under Art. III (c) of the Convention; they should be held criminally responsible for it (A/80/337, para. 70). The Commission examined and rejected counter-arguments: that on 10 October 2023 Gallant applied the term “human animals” to Hamas fighters (para. 230) and that Herzog did not expressly call for genocide (para. 231). → The statements verbatim

The obligation of all others

On notice since 26 January 2024

What the Commission tells States

Since at least the first order of the International Court of Justice on 26 January 2024, all States have been on notice of a serious risk of genocide; this triggered their obligation to prevent it with all means available (para. 249). The Commission recommends ceasing the transfer of arms and other equipment to Israel where there is reason to suspect that they could be used in acts of genocide (para. 257 b).

And Germany?

Since 7 October 2023 Germany has approved arms exports to Israel worth at least €1.4 billion – of which €508.75 million in weapons of war in the second quarter of 2026 alone – and has to defend itself before the International Court of Justice against the accusation of aiding and abetting. → The arms deliveries · → The proceedings

Fair in both directions – and checked

Who disputes the finding – and with what arguments

Each point first states the counter-position with its source, then checks it against court decisions, UN reports, human rights organisations and the scholarly literature. The academic objections are a legitimate part of the debate – where they are right, this is said explicitly.

  • 1The Israeli government Check: not supported

    “categorically” rejected the report on 16 September 2025, called it “distorted and false”, accused the authors of acting as proxies of Hamas and demanded that the Commission be abolished (Foreign Ministry, according to Reuters via Channels TV, press report). On 27 October 2026 Israel’s mission in Geneva called the follow-up report part of a “misinformation campaign” (Reuters via Business Day, press report).

    No rebuttal of the individual findings is known; Israel does not cooperate with the Commission (NPR, 6 August 2026). The Commission supports each element with references (A/HRC/60/CRP.3); its chair Srinivasan Muralidhar stated on 26 October 2026 that the evidence was gathered under protocols aligned with those of the ICJ and the ICC, and that the Commission would hand it to any court (The Wire, 27 October 2026, press report). No evidence is known for the claim that the authors act on behalf of Hamas – the Commission itself has established crimes by Hamas (point 8).

  • 2Self-defence: Check: oversimplified

    before the ICJ, Israel relies on the merits on self-defence against Hamas (Middle East Eye, 13 March 2026, press report).

    It is true that Israel was attacked on 7 October 2023, and the UN Commission itself has established the crimes of that day. As an answer to the genocide allegation, however, the argument does not hold: under Article I of the Convention genocide is prohibited “in time of peace or in time of war” – there is no justification. Self-defence is a right to wage war, not a licence in war. Moreover, the ICJ held in 2004 that Article 51 of the UN Charter presupposes an attack by a State and does not apply to threats from a territory that Israel itself controls (Advisory Opinion of 9 July 2004, para. 139; three judges – Higgins, Kooijmans and Buergenthal – disagreed with this reading in separate opinions). For Gaza, the ICJ found in 2024 that Israel’s obligations under the law of occupation continued after the 2005 withdrawal, commensurate with its effective control (Advisory Opinion of 19 July 2024, paras. 93–94). See the box below.

  • 3Jurisdiction: Check: not yet decided

    in its Counter-Memorial of 12 March 2026 Israel disputes the ICJ’s jurisdiction and the admissibility of the application (ICJ order of 21 May 2026, case 192).

    In January 2024 the ICJ found that it has jurisdiction under Article IX of the Convention prima facie – otherwise it could not have ordered provisional measures. It will probably decide finally together with the merits. South Africa considers the objection late, because Israel did not raise it within three months of the Memorial (South African Presidency, 3 June 2026).

  • 4The United States Check: partly accurate

    intervened in the ICJ case on Israel’s side on 12 March 2026, called the genocide allegation “false” and calls for a strict threshold for proving intent (Middle East Eye, 13 March 2026, press report). On 26 October 2026 it called the Commission’s findings “baseless” (US Mission to the UN).

    The strict threshold is the law and the strongest counter-argument: the ICJ requires “fully conclusive” evidence (Bosnia judgment 2007, para. 209); a pattern of conduct proves intent only if it can point to nothing else (para. 373). In Croatia v. Serbia the ICJ dismissed the claim in 2015 because the acts could have aimed at expulsion rather than destruction (case 118). But the UN Commission applied exactly this standard – “the only reasonable inference” (CRP.3, para. 254). And Germany, France, the United Kingdom and three other States argued in The Gambia v. Myanmar that only alternative explanations supported by evidence rule out the inference of intent (joint declaration, 15 November 2023, para. 52). That the allegation is “false” is a party’s assertion; the United States has presented no evidence to the contrary.

  • 5The counter-letter of more than 500 academics and practitioners Check: partly accurate

    (Academic Engagement Network, 9 September 2025) demands that the IAGS resolution be withdrawn: it is a “clear misapplication of law and history”, only 129 of more than 500 members voted, it erases the role of Hamas, which uses civilian facilities for military purposes, and it misrepresents the rulings of the ICJ and ICC (AEN press release).

    The point about the ICJ is justified: the resolution says the Court found it “plausible” “that Israel is committing genocide” (IAGS resolution) – that is inaccurate; what the Court found plausible was the right to protection (point 7). It is also true that only part of the membership voted. Not tenable is the claim in the release that the IAGS is silent on 7 October: the very first sentence of the resolution names the “horrific Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023, which itself constitutes international crimes”. And the letter targets the resolution, not the detailed evidentiary analyses of the UN Commission (2025), Amnesty (12/2024) or B'Tselem and PHRI (7/2025); their findings are unaffected.

  • 6The Israeli historian Benny Morris Check: partly accurate

    wrote on 19 June 2025: “Israel did not commit genocide and is not committing genocide in Gaza.” Genocide, he argues, is a state project planned from the top; no such plan exists. At the same time he concedes: “Yes, there have been, probably many, Israeli war crimes during the 19 months of the Gaza War” (Morris, “Dehumanization and genocide”).

    No written plan of destruction is indeed known, and Morris openly acknowledges “probably many” war crimes. But the Convention does not require a plan: intent may be inferred from statements and the pattern of conduct (Bosnia judgment, para. 373; Croatia 2015). The UN Commission relies on statements by Netanyahu, Herzog and Gallant as direct evidence (CRP.3, para. 254; → Intent). Other Israeli scholars reach the opposite conclusion, for example the Holocaust historian Omer Bartov (peer-reviewed article, 2025). The scholarly debate with both sides is on Wars & the genocide question.

  • 7The starting question at the ICJ: Check: accurate – but no exoneration

    the then President of the Court, Joan Donoghue, made clear in April 2024: “But the shorthand that often appears which is that there is a plausible case of genocide isn't what the court decided.” What is plausible is the Palestinians’ right to be protected from genocide (BBC HARDtalk, 26 April 2024; according to the Telegraph via Yahoo News, press report).

    The clarification is correct and also applies to supporters who misquote the ICJ (point 5). But it does not say that there is no genocide: the ICJ saw a real and imminent risk of irreparable harm to that right, relied on facts from UN sources (order of 26 January 2024, paras. 46–53) and ordered further measures on 28 March and 24 May 2024. Whether Israel has breached the Convention will only be decided on the merits (→ assessment).

  • 8Allegation of bias: Check: refuted

    the Commission ignores Hamas – so Israel (point 1) and, on 26 October 2026, Romania in the UN Third Committee (according to The Wire, press report).

    The Commission found war crimes by Hamas and other armed groups in relation to 7 October 2023 (A/HRC/56/CRP.3, 2024), investigated crimes by non-State actors including killings by forces close to Hamas (A/HRC/62/22, 2026) and, in report A/81/430, also examined killings by security forces of the Palestinian Authority (paras. 62–76).

International law: self-defence has limits – preventive force is prohibited
  • Prohibition of force: the UN Charter prohibits force against other States (Art. 2(4)). It is permitted only with a mandate from the Security Council or as self-defence against an armed attack (Art. 51).
  • Preventive attacks against threats that are not imminent are not covered by Article 51; according to the prevailing view they are unlawful. Justifications based on future threats – for buffer zones or a permanent military presence, for example – therefore do not hold.
  • Occupied territory: according to the ICJ’s 2004 Advisory Opinion, Article 51 is not relevant to threats from a territory that Israel itself controls (para. 139).
  • No licence: even those who defend themselves lawfully remain bound by international humanitarian law. The prohibition of genocide applies without exception, in peace and in war (Article I of the Convention).

Where the proceedings stand

12 Mar 2026

ICJ, South Africa v. Israel: Israel files its counter-memorial, disputing the Court’s jurisdiction. On the same day Namibia, the USA, Hungary and Fiji join the proceedings.

21 May 2026

New time limits: South Africa’s reply by 22 November 2027, Israel’s rejoinder by 22 May 2029 – only then oral hearings (ICJ order of 21 May 2026).

21 Jul 2026

New report by UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese to the General Assembly (A/81/249, “Truth under the rubble”): almost 300 journalists killed; the destruction of the media – “mediacide” – is, she says, part of the ongoing genocide. The USA maintains its sanctions against Albanese.

24 Jul 2026

International Criminal Court: The States parties remove Prosecutor Karim Khan for misconduct – unrelated to Gaza. The arrest warrants against Netanyahu and Gallant (21 November 2024, war crimes and crimes against humanity, not genocide) remain in force; Israel’s challenge to jurisdiction has not been decided.

2 Sep 2026

The UN Committee on the Exercise of the Inalienable Rights of the Palestinian People demands that Israel ensure its forces commit “no further genocidal acts” (translated from German) (A/81/35). There is no resolution of the General Assembly, the Security Council or the Human Rights Council with a finding of genocide; UN Secretary-General Guterres and High Commissioner Türk do not use the term.

7–10 Sep 2026

Nicaragua v. Germany: hearings on Germany’s objections to jurisdiction; judgment pending. → Details

18 Sep 2026

Colombia withdraws its participation in the ICJ case; 21 States remain registered.

9 Oct 2026

Nobel Peace Prize for Navi Pillay, until 2025 chair of the Commission of Inquiry; the Nobel Committee expressly honours this chairmanship. Israel’s Foreign Ministry sharply attacked the decision. On the same day the USA imposes sanctions on the International Criminal Court as a whole; Guterres calls this “a serious blow” to international criminal justice.

26 Oct 2026

New report by the UN Commission of Inquiry (A/81/430 of 14 October 2026): Israeli authorities and security forces in Gaza are continuing to commit acts of genocide even after the ceasefire with intent to destroy (paras. 54, 59); for the West Bank it finds crimes against humanity, not genocide. The report also examines killings by security forces of the Palestinian Authority (paras. 62–76). Israel speaks of a “misinformation campaign”, the USA rejects “all its baseless findings” (US Mission, 26 October 2026). → Assessment

19 Nov 2026

ICJ, The Gambia v. Myanmar – judgment: Seven years after the application, the Court finds that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the 2017 “clearance operations” in northern Rakhine. Myanmar also breached its duties to prevent and punish genocide and the provisional measures order of January 2020; the claim of direct and public incitement was rejected. It is the first time the ICJ has found a State responsible for the commission of genocide; Germany was an intervener (ICJ press release, UN News). Reports give differing vote counts, so none is given here.

Since 27 November 2025 the Commission of Inquiry has been chaired by Srinivasan Muralidhar, with Florence Mumba and Chris Sidoti. Checked on 16 November 2026: in South Africa v. Israel and Nicaragua v. Germany there has been no new document since September 2026; the ICJ press releases since late October (2026/25 to 2026/29) concern other matters (ICJ, press releases). No new resolution of the General Assembly, the Security Council or the Human Rights Council on the genocide question has been found since October 2026.
